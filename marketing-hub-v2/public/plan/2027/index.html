03 · Strategic principles

Four principles behind every campaign

Principle 1

Commercial first

Campaigns begin with what the business wants customers to do.

  • Book a transatlantic sailing
  • Reserve capacity earlier
  • Contact us about a yacht purchased overseas
  • Engage Forwarding for an air, sea or road freight requirement
  • Involve CMT early in a complex project
  • Involve Racing before a campaign's logistics plan is fixed
Principle 2

Solution-led

Start with the customer's requirement rather than forcing them into a particular transport product. Talk about:

  • The problem
  • The decision
  • The options
  • The consequences
  • Peters & May's solution

not simply the mode of transport.

Principle 3

Proof over claims

Use evidence instead of generic statements about being global, reliable or experienced.

Real shipmentsProjectsTechnical detailCustomer questionsStaff expertiseOperational footageTestimonialsNamed routesCase studiesUseful numbers
Principle 4

Quality over volume

Marketing does not need to fill every channel every day. One strong project can generate:

Case studyPRSocialShort videoSEO contentAI-search answerSales collateralPartner content

The objective is to make good material work harder.

Why these four

Why these four strategies?

The 2027 Marketing Plan is organised around four areas with different commercial opportunities, audiences, buying cycles and marketing requirements.

The purpose is not to create four separate Marketing departments. It is to give each part of the business the right strategy while bringing all activity together through one Group Marketing calendar.

Yacht Transport

Protect and strengthen the core business

Yacht Transport remains one of the Group's established core specialisms.

Its marketing is strongly influenced by seasonal yacht movements, sailing schedules, established trade routes, new-build activity and the international boat-show calendar.

The strategy therefore focuses on
Supporting priority sailings and routesEncouraging earlier customer planningStrengthening broker, dealer and builder relationshipsInternational yacht sales and relocationNew-build deliveryLarger and more complex yacht movementsRepeat business and customer retentionUsing real shipment activity as proof

Briese sits within Yacht Transport as an always-on route and partnership campaign rather than a separate strategic division.

Racing

Grow a specialist international market

Racing requires a distinct strategy because the audience, buying cycle and logistical requirements differ from mainstream Yacht Transport.

Teams may require a combination of
Yacht transportContainersEquipment movementsFreightCustomsTime-critical deliveriesRace-to-race logisticsYard support

Planning often begins months before the event itself.

The 2027 strategy will focus on developing relationships with teams and the wider Racing supply chain, alongside opportunities such as the Admiral's Cup and Rolex Fastnet Race, maxi campaigns, one-design classes and regattas, and other relevant programmes.

Freight Forwarding

Strengthen a substantial part of the Group and grow its wider opportunity

Freight Forwarding is already a substantial part of Peters & May's business and should be treated as a major strategic marketing stream in its own right.

It serves a broader range of commercial customers and sectors than Yacht Transport and operates with a different sales cycle, customer journey and set of priorities.

Marketing should support the existing business and further growth, led by energy and industrial, through
Energy: oil and gas, renewables, powerIndustrial and manufacturing customersHeavy machinery and project cargoAutomotiveMotorsportManufacturers and suppliersAir freightRoad freightSea freightCustomsSpecialist and time-critical movementsRacing-related freightMarine supply chainsGreater use of Peters & May's international office network

Rather than following a seasonal yacht calendar, Forwarding marketing should operate around agreed quarterly commercial priorities and key sectors.

Courier

Courier also sits within the wider Freight Forwarding proposition.

A rebrand bringing CEC into the Peters & May brand is coming in the future, creating a more joined-up proposition and greater opportunity for cross-selling.

The rebrand is a separate project. It will be added to the Marketing calendar once timing and scope are confirmed; until then, Marketing continues to support the Courier business as it stands.

Commercial Marine Transport

Build authority in specialist project markets

Commercial Marine operates in a technically led, project-based market where experience, capability and industry relationships strongly influence purchasing decisions.

Marketing should therefore concentrate on quality and authority rather than content volume.

Priority areas
Heavy liftLo-Lo and Flo-FloSemi-submersible transportTowageWorkboatsCTVsOffshore craftCommercial vesselsSpecialist marine projects

Strong technical case studies, project evidence, expert commentary and targeted industry activity will be more valuable than generic promotion.

How other activities fit

Not every service, sector, region or partnership requires its own Marketing strategy.

Briese
An always-on Yacht Transport campaign and strategic partnership.
Courier / CEC
Part of the wider Forwarding proposition. A rebrand bringing CEC into Peters & May is coming in the future, run as a separate project.
Asia and the Middle East
Geographic opportunities that run through the relevant divisions rather than standalone Marketing strategies.
Partnerships, affiliations and sponsorships
A shared Group workstream supporting all four strategies.
Group Marketing
Shared activity including PR, SEO, AI search, paid media, content, filming, LinkedIn Newsletter, events, CRM and measurement.

Commercial measurement

Rather than introducing an unverified Group conversion figure, each strategy should establish its own commercial baseline using the most reliable information available.

Where possible, Marketing should work towards measuring
Qualified opportunitiesQuotationsConfirmed businessCampaign-attributed leadsPartner referralsRepeat businessRevenue contribution, where availableReasons opportunities are won or lost

The priority is to improve the quality of commercial measurement rather than introduce headline conversion figures that cannot yet be consistently verified.

04 · Strategy 1 · Yacht Transport

Global Yacht Transport

Commercial situation

Yacht Transport remains the most seasonal part of the plan. The market is becoming more selective, with customers often committing nearer to departure and a greater share of value coming from larger or more complex movements.

Marketing therefore needs to:

  • Create demand earlier
  • Support conversion of existing enquiries
  • Communicate real capacity
  • Demonstrate why transport is valuable
  • Support brokers and dealers as well as owners
  • Build confidence through operational proof
  • Promote the right route at the right point in the season

Yacht ownersCaptainsBrokersDealersBuildersYardsCharter managersNew-build teamsFamily offices and representatives, where relevant

International yacht sales

Yacht location should not unnecessarily restrict the buyer market. Transport can be part of the sales strategy.

New-build delivery

Technical confidence

24 · Request management

A request should not automatically create another campaign

When somebody asks Marketing for new activity, run it through four questions.

Question 1

Does this fit the existing monthly or quarterly campaign?

Yes: include it.

Question 2

Is it commercially more important than something already scheduled?

Yes: replace or reprioritise.

Question 3

Is there a genuine time-sensitive opportunity?

Yes: use flexible capacity.

Default

Otherwise

Backlog it.

2. Approach partners before the campaign starts

For each major campaign, ask: who already has access to the audience we want to reach? Then approach them before activity begins.

Eastbound Transatlantic

Brokers, marinas, Caribbean organisations, yards, ARC / World Cruising Club, US offices, relevant shipping partners.

  • Can we create a joint guide?
  • Can you share our sailing information?
  • Can we provide useful content for your customers?
  • Can we run a webinar or Q&A?
  • Can we feature a customer story together?
  • Can we cross-link relevant website content?

Briese

Rather than simply publishing schedules:

  • Agree a joint quarterly campaign plan
  • Exchange usable imagery
  • Create route and project case studies
  • Coordinate sailing announcements
  • Secure links from partner websites
  • Create builder and dealer-facing information
  • Identify PR opportunities together

Racing: Admiral's Cup and Fastnet

The organisers and official logistics partnerships sit with competitors, so go to the teams directly. Approach teams, owners, team managers, suppliers, sailmakers and yards months before the event, and position Peters & May as the independent specialist that teams can choose.

  • Direct outreach to entered and likely teams
  • Logistics guidance for international teams
  • Pre-event freight checklist
  • Customs guidance
  • Team case study
  • Behind-the-scenes content with our own teams
  • Referrals through suppliers, sailmakers and yards

Avoid anything that implies an official event partnership.

5. Proactively identify content

At the start of each month, ask specific teams targeted questions linked to the campaign:

  • What unusual shipment is coming?
  • What technically difficult project is being planned?
  • What customer question keeps appearing?
  • What yacht is moving on this route?
  • What Racing project is coming?
  • What Forwarding job demonstrates our network?
  • What could we photograph or film?
  • What would make a good customer story?

Avoid asking: "Does anyone have any content?"

Future sponsorship criteria

Do not add sponsorships simply because an organisation or event approaches us. A potential sponsorship should meet several of these tests:

  • Audience: does it reach people we genuinely want to speak to?
  • Commercial relevance: can it influence enquiries, relationships or bookings?
  • Access: do we get meaningful access to members, customers, teams or participants?
  • Content: can it generate useful stories, video, expert content or guidance?
  • Digital value: website links, newsletter visibility or search authority?
  • Referral potential: could the organisation actively refer customers?
  • Exclusivity: do we have a meaningful position against competitors?
  • Measurement: can we identify what we receive in return?

If the answer is mainly "logo visibility", challenge it.
